Project Overview

Mobile Computer Workstation

A rolling, height-adjustable cart carrying a permanent HP OMEN desktop that also accepts a docked laptop. Both share one monitor, one keyboard and mouse, one network connection, and one power cord. Roll it up, connect a laptop with a single USB-C cable, and switch between systems without rewiring anything.

What this cart does

A self-contained mobile workstation. It carries a permanent HP OMEN desktop and accepts a laptop on the arm. The monitor, keyboard, mouse, network, and shared USB devices move between the two.

✓
One-cable laptop connection
A compatible laptop connects to the Anker Nano dock with one detachable USB-C cable carrying video, Ethernet, USB, and up to 100W of charging.
✓
Full monitor performance, no video switch
All three sources plug directly into the monitor's own inputs. The desktop runs at 4K and 165 Hz with G-Sync intact, which an HDMI switch would have capped at 60 Hz.
✓
One flat network
The travel router runs in bridge mode, so cart devices get addresses from the house router. AirPlay to the Apple TV works from any phone in the house.
✓
One cord to unplug
A single power cord feeds an on-board strip. Lower the cart, unplug one cord, and roll.

How to use it

Use the desktop

Select DisplayPort on the monitor, then select the HP OMEN on the USB switch.

Use a laptop

Mount the laptop on the arm and connect the one USB-C cable to the Anker Nano dock. Select HDMI 1 on the monitor, then select Laptop on the USB switch.

Use the Apple TV

Select HDMI 2 on the monitor. The Apple TV stays on the house network and uses its own remote.

Move the cart

Lower the cart fully, unplug the single power cord, then unlock the casters. Always lower before moving.

Verify before ordering

Four things to confirm before spending money. The first two could not be settled from published specifications and could change what you buy; the last two are tests to run once the parts arrive.

1
Does the cart's edge accept a C-clamp?
Both the monitor arm and the laptop arm clamp to the work surface. Measure the edge thickness and confirm no frame lip blocks a clamp within 2 inches of the edge. VIVO does not publish the edge profile. If it fails, the cart must change or the monitor goes back on its own stand.
2
Does the router bridge its Ethernet port?
Set Client mode, plug a laptop into the LAN port, and confirm it gets an address from the house router — not a 192.168.0.x of the TL-WR3002X's own making. If it does not, try Range Extender mode. Avoid Hotspot/WISP: it connects to the house Wi-Fi and looks correct, but adds NAT, and AirPlay fails silently while everything else keeps working.
3
Does each Windows laptop support USB-C video and charging?
DisplayPort Alt Mode and Power Delivery vary by machine. Test before relying on the one-cable path.
4
Does the dock-to-USB-switch chain hold up?
This puts a hub behind a hub. Test the keyboard, mouse, and any shared device before mounting anything permanently.

Quick glossary

Bridge mode: A router setting that passes devices through to the existing network instead of creating a second, separate one.

mDNS: The discovery protocol behind AirPlay and HomeKit. It does not cross between networks, which is why bridge mode matters here.

USB switch: A selector that moves shared USB devices such as the keyboard dongle between two computers, without touching video.

PD: USB Power Delivery, the standard used to charge the laptop over USB-C.

G-Sync: Syncs the monitor's refresh rate to the graphics card to remove tearing. Video switches often break it.

PoE: Power over Ethernet. Lets a network device draw power from its data cable. Not used in this build.
